# Outdoor spatial AR (experimental)

The backend prepares recorded geographic evidence for a local guardian renderer. The guardian device owns the camera session. Google-assisted alignment optionally processes guardian sensor data through Google after explicit opt-in. The wearer phone/watch do not record or upload images. The PC performs coordinate geometry and preserves the accuracy limits of the original observations.

## Indoor limits

Geographic spatial AR is an experimental outdoor mode. The separate **Camera + motion diagram (no indoor AR)** shows a local camera and a relative research diagram; it never projects that diagram onto the floor. Starting room, floor, north and the transform into the guardian's camera remain unknown. A freely pocketed wearer phone records no visual landmarks, so merely walking around does not build a visual map that another phone can recognize. IMU/watch observations alone do not provide that shared reference. Universal indoor localization or navigation with only a pocketed phone, without mapping or another usable reference, is not implemented.

Relative motion recording needs no doorway, fixed-phone or straight-walking calibration. The outdoor registration walk below serves a different purpose: relating geographic evidence to the guardian camera. Measured waypoint tests in the [motion guide](MOTION_EVALUATION.md#measured-comparison) evaluate accuracy after recording; they are not a required wearer setup step.

## API

`GET /api/sessions/:id/ar`, with the existing bearer credential, returns a version-1 geographic frame and separate source paths. It requires an active assistance request (`alert`) and guardian or owner access. Owner access is for inspecting their own evidence; it does not enable a wearer camera screen. Watch access is rejected. A separate simulated session with an active shared demo walk may also request its generated AR evidence before escalation. Other requests before escalation and all requests after resolution return 409; revoked/expired credentials return 401 and deleted sessions return 404. POST and other mutations are rejected. Reading AR evidence does not acknowledge, check in, resolve or extend a session.

The frame uses WGS84 and meters: `x` east, `y` up, `z` south, compatible with a right-handed camera world after an explicit registration. The earliest usable observed fix identifies the anchor. A late earlier fix can change `frame.anchor.id`: the client must clear its previous registration if that ID changes.

Paths retain phone/watch provenance and time/disagreement gaps. For each source, the most accurate non-mock observation per second is selected before geometry is built. Mock fixes, zero/unknown precision, reported horizontal uncertainty above 25 m, future observations and impossible jumps are excluded. A rejected jump does not become the baseline for the next check. Excluded observations break the line. Raw readings remain available in geographic evidence. The map uses separate `mapPaths` that omit movement within reported precision, preserving each section's first and latest endpoint. Its camera bounds exclude rejected outliers. The local AR frame is limited to 2 km from its earliest accepted anchor. Neither view fills gaps, averages correlated phone/watch locations or creates coordinates from steps/pressure.

Height is `null` unless both anchor and observation report vertical accuracy within 10 m and come from the same source. Known height is only a relative altitude observation, with the sum of both reported vertical uncertainties. Horizontal uncertainty also includes the anchor's reported uncertainty. These sums are conservative allowances, not calibrated confidence intervals. No floor, wall, room or stair geometry is inferred.

New Android phone/watch altitude observations explicitly declare `altitudeDatum: WGS84-ellipsoid` (Android `Location.getAltitude`). The API preserves missing datum on legacy observations so queued retries remain idempotent. AR points declare a compatible datum only when both heights declare it. Legacy observations can support a horizontal camera guide without claiming a measured height.

## Local registration and rendering

Frames explicitly have `registrationRequired: true` and `cameraAligned: false`; every packet has `guidanceAllowed: false`. A current usable last fix yields `fallback: alignment-required`; absent, stale, poor or distant current evidence requires the map. An available historical path does not mean the wearer remains at its last point.

The Flutter guardian screen hosts native **ARKit with RealityKit** on iPhone, and **ARCore 1.57** on Android. Android AR is optional; unsupported phones open the GPS + compass camera guide and retain the map. Local alignment does not configure cloud processing or image upload. Android also offers optional Google-assisted Geospatial alignment, described below. Guardian location and pose samples remain in memory on that phone. Wearer recording, queues, check-ins, escalation and haptics have no dependency on AR.

Registration pairs a fresh guardian geographic fix with the camera pose at the same instant (maximum 200 ms difference). It projects guardian fixes into the API's identical WGS84 tangent frame, fits a gravity-preserving horizontal rigid transform from an outdoor walking baseline, and freezes that transform for checks against later fixes. This derives yaw from measured movement, without compass heading. The endpoints must span at least 30 m and eight times their summed reported horizontal accuracy; guardian fixes must report 5 m accuracy or better. Three later checks, separated by at least two seconds and two meters of camera movement, must agree within 5 m and the registration allowances. They are withheld checks from the same location system, **not an independent reference or field accuracy proof**.

Measured vertical registration requires guardian ellipsoid altitude with reported vertical accuracy within 5 m and a compatible recorded anchor. Missing, incompatible or overly uncertain heights now use an explicitly labeled **floating horizontal guide**, placed on a presentation plane 1.3 m below the initial camera pose. This plane is not recorded altitude, detected ground, terrain, a floor or a stair. Its `heightMode: horizontal` render points have no vertical uncertainty bar. A missing legacy altitude datum no longer prevents opening the camera or following horizontal observations.

Rendering is restricted to observations within 50 m with combined horizontal allowances no greater than 25 m. Measured vertical geometry additionally requires an allowance no greater than 10 m. Cyan marks phone evidence; orange marks watch evidence, which may come from the paired phone. Horizontal rings depict uncertainty; vertical bars appear only for measured heights. A ribbon and chevrons connect points in recording order within each continuous source/height segment. Excluded points, source changes and measured/horizontal height transitions break the ribbon. Geometry has no validated wall/obstacle occlusion and cannot establish a passable route through buildings or stairs. A registration with no supported points returns to the map.

Native camera poses update an on-camera direction arrow and approximate next-point distance at up to 10 Hz. The guide starts with the nearest supported phone point (watch when phone points are unavailable), advances within 3 m, and never moves backward or automatically jumps a source or section gap. Range clipping can retain progress only when the same target observation remains visible. Within combined horizontal uncertainty, the arrow becomes a search indicator. Reaching the final supported point never verifies arrival or resolves the assistance request.

Allowances include recorded fix accuracy, guardian endpoint uncertainty, endpoint length disagreement, angular uncertainty, worst later residual and an engineering drift allowance of 2% of cumulative camera travel. The anchor's geographic offset cancels in the horizontal rigid fit; its reported measurement error is not counted again on top of the recorded fix and guardian errors. Legacy render packets without per-fix accuracy use the more conservative relative allowance. These are experimental thresholds, not calibrated confidence intervals or a guarantee of physical error. Registration expires after 120 seconds or 100 m of further camera travel; acquisition is bounded to 180 seconds/200 m. Any camera tracking loss after tracking starts, backgrounding, permission/access loss, changed frame anchor, stale/poor location or failed check closes AR and requires reopening and re-registration. Native render leases also expire after 2.5 seconds without Flutter updates. Startup without usable synchronized location times out after 15 seconds.

The backend cannot observe or validate a particular camera session, so its `cameraAligned: false`, `registrationRequired: true` and `guidanceAllowed: false` remain unchanged. A successful local consistency check enables only that device's experimental recorded-trail guide. The separate camera compass preview is still unregistered.

## Optional Google-assisted alignment on Android

Opening the live/shared-demo camera offers **Use local alignment** or **Agree & use Google**. The latter presents the required disclosure that Google processes camera and location sensor data, with a link to its data-handling information. It enables only ARCore Geospatial; Maps routing, Places, paid map tiles, terrain and rooftop resolution are not configured. The local camera animation never enables Geospatial. Camera audio and recording remain disabled. Google-assisted mode changes the guardian data flow; KAZE's backend does not receive guardian camera images or localization samples.

Keyless authorization uses an Android OAuth client with package `app.kaze.trail` and the actual APK signing certificate SHA-1. Follow [Android ARCore authorization](https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/authorization?platform=android): create a Google Cloud project without linking billing, enable **ARCore API**, then create an Android OAuth client in Credentials. Obtain the debug certificate with `cd mobile/android` and `./gradlew signingReport` (`gradlew.bat` on Windows). Each release signing certificate needs its own client registration. No API key, client secret or service-account file is embedded in the application. If setup requires billing, do not enable it for a free-only deployment. Google currently publishes [Geospatial quotas](https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/java/geospatial/api-usage-quota); review terms before distribution rather than treating a quota as a perpetual pricing guarantee.

The native renderer checks Geospatial device support before enabling it. Each tracked Earth pose supplies the camera location and a gravity-preserving east/up/south orientation in the same AR frame. The existing registration renderer accepts those transforms only with reported horizontal accuracy within 5 m and yaw accuracy within 10 degrees, and retains its 50 m range, 25 m combined uncertainty, separate source/gap and compatible-height gates. No walking baseline is required in this mode. Google localization estimates are not the local mode's three withheld consistency checks, independent physical accuracy proof, or proof that VPS contributed to a particular pose. Missing compatible heights use a presentation plane below the camera, not detected pavement. Geospatial cannot correct errors in the wearer's recorded route.

Acquisition is bounded to 60 seconds and draws no directions until the estimates are usable. Unsupported devices, API authorization/quota errors, camera tracking loss, loss of precise localization after acquisition, stale wearer evidence, permission/access loss or backgrounding close the session or expose the labeled compass fallback. Reopening with local alignment remains available. Real outdoor coverage and accuracy require physical testing after project authorization. iOS remains on the existing local ARKit path.

## Shared walking demo and camera guide

Enable **Demo mode** in Settings on both phones, then choose **Start shared walk** from the welcome screen with no active session. This creates a separate simulated session with live sharing; it never starts wearer recording. Pair a guardian through the existing invitation code or QR. **Settings → Shared walk** provides pause, resume, restart and 1x/2x/4x playback controls; only the owner can change playback. The guardian's **Open shared walk camera** opens approximate outdoor GPS/compass placement before an alert without the native AR registration walk. The optional spatial AR action retains its outdoor registration requirements. Both devices read the same server-clock movement, recorded trail and latest virtual wearer position. Restart creates a new run. Compass progress resets; native AR closes when the frame anchor changes and must be reopened. Ordinary playback controls never move the check-in deadline. The explicit alert-take control below resets only simulated deadlines; consent, authorization, revocation and expiry still apply.

Turning demo mode off hides the simulated session and all demo controls, closes a demo camera, and stops that phone's simulated guardian notifications. It does not end/delete the session, change pairing or deadlines, or stop live guardian alerts. Re-enabling can reveal the retained session again. The generic Settings entry remains available; limitation notices stay visible.

`POST /api/sessions/:id/demo-walk` accepts owner commands `start`, `pause`, `resume`, `restart`, and `speed`. Starting requires explicit consent and 2–40 route vertices spanning 10–2000 m. Generated fixes preserve each route corner and carry `mock: true`, `simulated: true`, and `provider: kaze-demo`; only explicitly simulated sessions admit them to map/AR geometry. They are never presented as physical measurements. Full route state stays server-side; snapshots expose only traveled evidence and playback status. A private `KAZE_DEMO_ROUTE` build define can hold a JSON route, with optional street names, without committing private coordinates. Without a preset, the app creates a synthetic nearby route from a precise foreground location; that route does not establish passable streets or sidewalks.

The full-screen camera guide shows a large direction arrow, next-point distance, available street label, and a small map with the guardian position. Native AR uses the local registration described above. Phones without native AR use a rear-camera preview with approximate GPS + compass projection. It requires fresh real guardian locations within 20 m reported accuracy, fresh wearer evidence, and an acceptable compass reading; Android magnetic headings are corrected for local declination. Moving or turning the guardian updates the projection and next-point cue. Geometry behind the camera is clipped without breaking walking progress. Rejected observations and source gaps still break the trail. Within combined location uncertainty, a search indicator replaces the direction arrow. This mode has no visual localization, ground detection, measured height or obstacle avoidance; camera placement can drift with GPS and compass errors. Camera images are not uploaded. Google-assisted alignment is available separately on compatible Android phones after opt-in and project authorization.

For a repeatable missed check-in video, the owner can press **Start 40-second alert take**. This explicitly restarts an active simulated route at 1x, sets a 30-second check-in interval and 10-second grace, clears the previous simulated response state, and pauses the virtual wearer at the forty-second deadline. Pairing and expiry stay unchanged. Ordinary playback controls never reset a deadline, and the filming control cannot reset a real wearer session. See [filming instructions](DEMO_FILMING.md) for iPhone builds, camera limitations and the recording sequence.

## Local AR demonstration

Enable demo mode and choose **Try guardian camera demo** from the welcome screen or **Settings → Device setup** during a guardian session. This opens a camera-only demonstration without requiring an assistance request, another wearer, a backend connection, GPS, or the live trail's walking registration. Point toward a well-lit open space. **Start walk** moves a pink virtual wearer along an 18 m route with two turns at approximately 1.2 m/s. **Pause walk**, **Resume walk**, **Restart walk** and **1x/2x speed** control playback. The pink trail is generated only behind the moving figure. On supported devices with installed AR services, camera movement comes from ARKit/ARCore tracking and geometry stays anchored in the initial camera world.

When native AR is unavailable or still checking, the demo opens a regular camera preview with a perspective animation and an explicit phone-movement limitation. This fallback works on phones that cannot start ARCore, including the tested Vivo. Its virtual viewpoint is fixed: moving the real phone does not move the simulated viewpoint or register the trail to the room. The fallback is a presentation aid, not spatial navigation. Camera/microphone recording, image streaming and frame upload are never started; camera audio is disabled.

All positions and the presentation height are synthetic local metres. The demo never fetches incident evidence, uploads events, changes check-ins or alerts, or saves a capture. Its synthetic source stays separate from phone/watch evidence; live AR continues to reject mock locations. Backgrounding and camera failure stop both modes. Native tracking loss and render-lease expiry stop the spatial mode. Android still requires ending active wearer recording before opening either camera demo. The demo requires a rear camera and camera permission; it does not establish live location accuracy or obstacle clearance. iPhone changes require a Mac/Xcode build and device verification.

## Verification

For device diagnostics, run the updated app from `mobile/` with `flutter run -d DEVICE_ID` on the connected phone's development machine (a Mac for iPhone). Preserve any `--dart-define=KAZE_BASE_URL=...` used for that build. Keep the terminal attached and reopen **Outdoor spatial AR (experimental)**. Lines prefixed `[KAZE AR]` report startup stages and the first closure reason. They omit session credentials, server addresses, geographic observations and camera images. The iPhone renderer forwards specific location, tracking, interruption and timeout failures to that same log; numeric native error codes are included without raw error descriptions. The closure reason also remains visible on the AR screen. These diagnostics require rebuilding and installing the updated app; older builds do not emit them.

`npm test` covers geographic geometry, provenance, datum validation/legacy retries, gaps and endpoint authorization/lifecycle. `cd mobile; flutter test` covers registration geometry, uncertainty rejection, privacy and screen lifecycle with simulated native channels. Android APK compilation/lint checks native API integration. These tests do **not** prove real camera alignment.

Remaining gates: signed Mac/Xcode build and permission testing on the iPhone 17 (reported iOS 26.6.2); simultaneous Vivo/watch/iPhone guardian operation; a consenting outdoor route with an independent surveyed/reference measurement, measured marker error and uncertainty coverage; physical tracking/registration/failure checks; stable HTTPS deployment before normal distribution. The current temporary tunnel is not a stable production host. Keep geographic/device artifacts ignored and never commit credentials or exact private coordinates.
